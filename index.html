Field guide for wildlife spotting across the Inside Passage and Salish Sea loops. Keep your 8x42 optics ready on deck and at the shoreline.
🐋Marine Mammals
⌄
Orca (Killer Whale) Resident & Transient
📍 Best Spots: Inside Passage, Semiahmoo Bay, San Juan Ferries
Field Marks: Jet-black body with a stark white eye patch and a gray saddle patch behind the dorsal fin. Adult males feature a towering, triangular dorsal fin up to 6 feet tall.
Behavior: Look for sharp, explosive blows. Transients travel in quiet, tactical hunting packs of 2–6, while Residents travel in larger, vocal family pods. Watch for breach or spy-hop sequences.
Humpback Whale Baleen Whale
📍 Best Spots: Inside Passage (southbound lanes), Juneau whale watching
Field Marks: Massive dark body with a prominent dorsal hump and long, white pectoral flippers easily visible through optics when rolling.
Behavior: Signals a deep dive by arching its back and throwing its wide tail fluke clear out of the water. Look for massive, bushy, 10-foot blows on glassy mornings.
Harbor Seal Pinniped
📍 Best Spots: Semiahmoo Spit, White Rock Pier, Naomi Cottage backyard narrows
Field Marks: Plump, silvery-gray or mottled brown body with no external ear flaps. "Bow-riding" or trailing behind kayaks.
Behavior: Pops up closely to docks, structures, and slow-moving watercraft like a floating bowling ball. Often hauls out in clusters on sandbars, log booms, or rocky reefs at lower tides.
🦅Coastal & Apex Avian Species
⌄
Bald Eagle Raptor
📍 Best Spots: Inside Passage sea days, Semiahmoo Spit old nests, Fidalgo Bay trestle
Field Marks: Imposing size, dark chocolate brown body contrasted against a pure white head and tail. Massive hook-shaped bright yellow bill.
Behavior: Scans for salmon from prominent, dead old-growth tree snags. Harassed frequently by crows or gulls when carrying a catch. Look high on coastal tree structures along narrow waterways.
Great Blue Heron Wader
📍 Best Spots: Birch Bay State Park tidepools, Semiahmoo Spit shallows
Field Marks: Large, tall frame with blue-gray plumage, a shaggy chest crest, and a long, dagger-like yellow bill. In flight, it tucks its neck into a tight 'S' shape.
Behavior: Stands perfectly motionless for long periods in shallow water or mudflats, tracking tiny fish or crabs before striking with blinding speed.
Pigeon Guillemot Alcid
📍 Best Spots: Larrabee State Park cliffs, Deception Pass rapids, ferry docks
Field Marks: Compact, soot-black body distinguished by sharp white wing patches with black wedges. Features bright, vivid coral-red legs and a red interior mouth lining.
Behavior: Dives efficiently in fast-moving water columns near sandstone cliffs and rocky drop-offs. Emits a high-pitched whistling squeak when aggregating on water surfaces.
🦀Intertidal & Tidal Flat Marine Life
⌄
Ochre Sea Star Invertebrate
📍 Best Spots: Birch Bay low tide, Centennial Sand Flats rocky outcrops
Field Marks: Robust, stiff five-armed star structure ranging in intense shades of deep purple, bright orange, or brown. Textured with hard, white calcareous spines.
Behavior: Clings tightly to wave-beaten rocks, barnacle clusters, and structural pilings in the mid-to-lower intertidal zones using rows of secure suction tube feet.
Pacific Dungeness & Red Rock Crab Crustacean
📍 Best Spots: Birch Bay mudflats, Boundary Bay pools
Field Marks: Dungeness has a wide, fan-shaped purplish-brown shell with white-tipped claws. Red Rock is smaller, thickset, uniform brick-red with distinct heavy black tips on its claws.
Behavior: Scurries sideways to bury itself backward into loose sand when exposed by receding tides. Found easily by turning over large pebbles or searching shallow eelgrass drainage paths.
